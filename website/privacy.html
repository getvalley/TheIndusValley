Your browsing. More control.

Valley uses a built-in domain blocklist and conservative third-party cookie defaults to reduce some common ad and tracking requests.

How it works

  1. A page requests content

    Scripts, images or other resources, often from different domains.

  2. Valley checks the host

    The domain is compared against the built-in blocklist.

  3. Known trackers are blocked

    Matching requests get an empty response instead of loading.

  4. Browsing continues

    Everything else loads as normal.

The protection layers

  • Known tracker blockingA built-in list of advertising, analytics and tracking domains. It is domain-based, so it cannot identify every technique.
  • Third-party cookies offDisabled by default in the WebView configuration. A setting can allow them if a site needs them.
  • Site exceptionsA domain on the allowed list, including its subdomains, is exempt from the blocklist.
  • Local counterA tally of blocked requests kept in local app preferences. A diagnostic, not proof that every tracker was stopped.

Know the boundaries

What it can do

Block requests to domains on the list. Reduce some third-party cookie tracking by default. Let you manage exceptions and cookie settings.

What it does not

Block every ad or tracker. Make you anonymous or hide your IP. Replace HTTPS, device security or safe habits. Stop fingerprinting or first-party storage.

Transparency note. This page describes the implementation in the Valley Android source. The blocklist can become outdated and websites change. Treat it as risk reduction, not anonymity, and avoid entering sensitive information on untrusted sites.
